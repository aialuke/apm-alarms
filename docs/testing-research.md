# Testing research

Source notes for the technician. One wired model and one wireless model per brand. Sources are manuals and official pages opened during this pass. This file is not a page on the phone. The field rule on every phone page is "Hold test button for 3 cycles of the alarm sound." (see the Testing section of the product brief). It wins over every hold time recorded below, which are manual facts only.

The test here is the button test. It shows that the alarm sounds, and that the interconnected alarms sound. A smoke spray test is included only when the sheet ties it to that button test. Factory reset and clearing a network stay in Flag. They are not test steps.

The Cavius field fact stays. After a test, every Cavius alarm chirps for about 2 minutes. That chirp is normal. The manual wait is written in the Cavius sections. The field fact is not replaced.

Remotes and RF modules are out of this pass.

## Cavius, wired alarm

**Result.** Found in a manual.

**Model.** The sheet prints model 2203-002 and part code 2901 CAVMPRK. The same page says this alarm can link with other 2203 CAVMP alarms. This is the wired guide named for 2203 CAVMP.

**Source.** Section 6, Test function, in the mains user guide. https://www.cavius.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2203-CAVMP-User-Guide-With-recess-kit.pdf

**Sheet line.** "Press the test button on any alarm for at least 10 seconds."

**How to start the test.** Press the test button on any alarm. The sheet says to do this after installation and at least once per quarter.

**How long to hold.** Hold the test button for at least 10 seconds.

**What this alarm does.** The alarm you press sends out a test signal. The sheet says the test also sends a weaker radio signal. The alarms then give a short beep, and the LED flashes every 8 seconds for 2 minutes. One beep every 8 seconds means the alarms are connected and working. Three short beeps every 8 seconds means a smoke sensor fault.

**What the other alarms do.** The other connected alarms should get the signal within a short time. The sheet then says "the alarms" give the short beep and the LED flash. It does not give those other alarms a different test sound.

**Wait after the test.** The short beep and the LED flash keep going every 8 seconds for 2 minutes. A short press of the test button stops that indication early on that alarm.

**Manual is silent on.** A separate action for the alarm you hold, once the signal is sent. The colour of the LED during those test beeps. The test section says "the LED". A wait after the alarm clicks onto a base.

**Flag.** After a test, every Cavius alarm chirps for about 2 minutes. That chirp is normal.

## Clipsal, wired alarm

**Result.** Found in a manual.

**Model.** 755WSA. The guide title is Smoke Alarm 240 V, works with Wiser.

**Source.** "Testing the device" on printed page 15, and the operating LED table on printed page 33. https://download.schneider-electric.com/files?p_Doc_Ref=755WSA_WH_DUG_EN&p_File_Name=755WSA_WH_DUG_EN.pdf

**Sheet line.** "Press and hold the Test/Hush button for more than 10 s until an alarm goes off on all interconnected devices."

**How to start the test.** The guide says to test once a month. In a mobile home, test every week and before every journey. For one device, press the Test/Hush button on that device. For a linked set, pick any linked device and press Test/Hush.

**How long to hold.** For one device, hold for more than 3 seconds, until an alarm sounds. For the linked set, hold for more than 10 seconds, until an alarm goes off on all the linked devices. The page marks each of these as one press. If there is no sound, the troubleshooting page says to check the power switch is on, then press Test/Hush until an alarm sounds. Do not press it consecutively.

**What this alarm does.** In test mode the standby LED blinks once every 1 second. The sound is 3 beeps every 4 seconds. Both keep going until you let go of Test/Hush. The LED table says the LED blinks red in every operating mode. If the one-device test makes no sound, the guide says the device is not connected to the power supply.

**What the other alarms do.** A hold longer than 10 seconds makes an alarm go off on all the linked devices. In the wireless interconnection test the standby LED stays off. The sound is 3 beeps every 4 seconds, and the table gives that sound 25 seconds. The note says the sound on those other devices keeps going for a longer time after you let go. The guide does not give a second number for that longer time.

**Wait after the test.** The table gives the other devices 25 seconds of sound. The test pages do not name another wait after this test.

**Manual is silent on.** A chirp after the test. A flash on the other alarms during the interconnection test. The hush pages say to wait 10 minutes before any test once the device is in hush mode. That line is not written as the end of a normal test.

## Clipsal, wireless alarm

**Result.** Found in a manual.

**Model.** 755LPSMA4. The sheet calls it a lithium battery photoelectric smoke alarm with wireless interconnect.

**Source.** The one-page installation guide, in "Testing interconnected smoke alarms", the red LED and sound table, and the monthly test lines. https://1300smokealarms.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Clipsal-755LPSMA4_Lithium_Battery_alarm_with_WI_Installation_Guide-1.pdf

**Sheet line.** "Press the Test/Hush button on each alarm for three seconds until the alarm sounds."

**How to start the test.** Press the Test/Hush button on each alarm. Test each linked alarm one by one. Do not use an open flame. The sheet says to test once a month. In a mobile home, test every week and before every journey.

**How long to hold.** Hold for three seconds, until the alarm sounds. Do not quick press. Also hold the Test button until the second burst of 3 beeps has finished.

**What this alarm does.** The test mode row shows one red flash every 1 second. The sound is 3 beeps every 4 seconds. Both last until you release Test/Hush. The sheet also says to hold until the alarm sounds, and until the second burst of 3 beeps has finished.

**What the other alarms do.** The alarm sends a wireless signal once you release Test/Hush. All the linked alarms sound for 10 seconds. They flash the front red LED twice a second for 5 minutes. The table calls that test 2 flashes every second, and continuous 2 beeps every 1 second. The duration line says 10 seconds for the alarm and 5 minutes for the LED. Hold Test/Hush on one alarm until it sounds, and check that the red LED is flashing on all the linked alarms. If that does not happen, check the wireless setup and the distance between alarms.

**Wait after the test.** After the first alarm, briefly press Test/Hush on the next alarm to stop the flashing red LED. Then hold Test/Hush again until that alarm sounds. Repeat this on every alarm. The other alarms can keep the red flash for 5 minutes. Their sound lasts 10 seconds.

**Manual is silent on.** A chirp after the test. A numbered wait after every test. In the hush section, the sheet says to wait 10 minutes before any more testing, so the alarm does not respond abnormally.

## GT, wired alarm

**Result.** Found in a manual.

**Model.** GT240. The same sheet also covers GT240L.

**Source.** GT240 user's manual. https://gtsmokealarms.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/GT240-MANUAL.pdf pdftotext returned no text from this PDF. The lines below are from the rendered pages of that file.

**Sheet line.** "Press and hold the Test/Silence button for 10 seconds, the alarm beeps."

**How to start the test.** Test the alarm once a week by pressing the Test/Silence button while it is working normally. The single alarm test starts by pressing the Test/Silence button. The interconnected test starts by pressing and holding the Test/Silence button on any interconnected alarm. After mains power is connected at installation, the check is also a press and hold of the Test/Silence button. If you test straight after the alarm is first activated, allow a few seconds for it to settle before the test.

**How long to hold.** The single alarm test does not give a hold time. The interconnected test says to hold until the other alarms in the network start to beep. The installation check says to hold the Test/Silence button for 10 seconds.

**What this alarm does.** On a single alarm, the red LED flashes once with a short beep. The buzzer then beeps 3 times continuously, and the red LED flashes 3 times, for two cycles. On an interconnected test, the initiating alarm beeps continuously. Its red LED flashes during that test. During the 10 second installation check, the alarm beeps. When that button is released, the buzzer stops at once.

**What the other alarms do.** The other interconnected alarms start to beep. Their lights flash red and yellow alternately. During the 10 second installation check, the other interconnected GT alarms also beep within that hold. When the button is released, the initiating alarm stops flashing and beeping. The other alarms stop testing soon after that.

**Wait after the test.** Not found.

**Manual is silent on.** A set number of seconds to hold for the single alarm test. A wait after the test. The test steps for the GTRFM wireless module. This sheet points to the GTRFM module manual for wireless interconnection.

**Flag.** The sheet says the warranty is void if the alarm is interconnected to any other brand or make of alarm.

## Legrand, wired alarm

**Result.** Found in a manual.

**Model.** Cat. No. 643088. The sheet calls it a mains powered alarm with wired or wireless interconnect.

**Source.** Legrand sheet for 643088. https://assets.legrand.com/pim/NP-FT-GT/INST%20SMOKE%20643088%20LE13408AE.pdf

**Sheet line.** "Push the Hush / Test button for 2-3 Seconds"

**How to start the test.** Test the alarms on a monthly basis. The sheet also says to use a suitable aerosol tester for the smoke sensing chamber. Before the button test, the alarm must be on the AC supply and the green LED must be on. The single alarm test starts by pushing the Hush/Test button. The alarm and network test starts by pushing the HUSH/TEST button on the RF or wired unit. The network range test starts by holding the Network button on one RF alarm in the network. Before that range test, the alarm must be on the AC supply and the green LED must be on.

**How long to hold.** The single alarm test is a push of 2 to 3 seconds. The alarm and network test is a push of 10 seconds. The horn keeps sounding until the button is released. The network range test is a hold of the Network button for 10 to 12 seconds. To leave that range test, hold the Network button on the master and count 2 to 3 blue flashes, then release.

**What this alarm does.** On the unit you push, the single test and the alarm and network test both give 3 beeps in 4 seconds for one cycle, repeating until the button is released. The red LED gives 3 flashes every 4 seconds for one cycle, repeating until the button is released. On the network range test, that alarm shows a solid blue light. Master mode times out in 9 minutes. When the range test is closed, the master blue light flashes fast.

**What the other alarms do.** Other interconnected RF units give 3 beeps in 2 seconds for one cycle, repeating until the button is released. Their blue LED flashes once every 4 seconds and stays on for 3 minutes. Other wired interconnected RF units give 3 beeps in 2 seconds for one cycle, repeating until the button is released. The LED columns for those wired units are marked N/A. On the range test, wait 30 seconds and then look at the other RF units. They show a slow blue flash. That indication also times out in 9 minutes. When the range test is closed, the blue lights on the slaves turn off.

**Wait after the test.** After the HUSH/TEST button test, the alarm is desensitized for about 20 minutes. Wait 20 minutes before the smoke aerosol test. Closing the network range test may take up to 90 seconds.

**Manual is silent on.** A sound from the alarm during the network range test. A test step for an alarm that is not a Legrand alarm.

**Flag.** The sheet has a factory reset that starts the alarm as if it had just been powered up, and that reset also removes it from the network. The sheet says the interconnect works only with other Legrand smoke alarms. The sheet says this alarm will be damaged if it is interconnected with another brand.

## Lifesaver, wired alarm

**Result.** Found in a manual.

**Model.** LIF6800. The same guide also covers 6800RL. The monthly test in section 8 is written for both. The notes below use that test, plus the battery test that sits before the 6800RL-only notes.

**Source.** PSA Lifesaver user guide, section 8 Testing the Smoke Alarm, and the Battery Test in section 7. https://www.psaproducts.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/1398-7216-03_UG_LIF6800_AUS_2026_SAI_V4.pdf

**Sheet line.** "Press and hold the TEST/Hush Button for at least five (5) seconds."
